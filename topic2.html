2. Scale and Impact of Forest Fires: Environmental, Economic and Human Losses

Wildfires can cause enormous environmental, economic and human losses every year. Globally, hundreds of millions of hectares of land can be affected by fire annually, although the exact amount varies substantially from year to year and depends on how fires are measured. The destruction goes beyond the area that burns directly. Forest fires can destroy forests, homes, infrastructure, crops and wildlife habitats, while also releasing greenhouse gases and large amounts of smoke.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), wildfire smoke can severely reduce air quality and increase respiratory and cardiovascular problems, hospital admissions and premature deaths. Economically, fires can result in billions of dollars of losses through destruction of property, infrastructure, agricultural land and forests, as well as firefighting and recovery costs. For example, a World Bank presentation using Munich Re data estimated that global forest fires caused approximately US$132.6 billion in economic losses between 2015 and 2024, including around US$78.6 billion in insured losses. The human cost can also be severe; the 2023 Maui wildfire, for example, resulted in at least 100 deaths and estimated economic losses of about US$5.6 billion.

Sources: World Health Organization (WHO), World Bank, Munich Re
